# Question

I am in a dispute with a builder over poor work carried out at my home. Could the policy pay my solicitor’s fees?

# Answer

No — the policy will not cover solicitor’s fees for a dispute that is simply a contract dispute with your builder unless other insured incident criteria apply.

Explanation:
- Work by a builder is a dispute arising from a contract you have entered into; Property disputes exclude “a contract you have entered into” [10].  
- Consumer (contract) disputes are covered only where they fall within the Consumer disputes wording and the amount in dispute is more than £100; however that section specifically excludes “a dispute over the sale, purchase, terms of a lease, licence, or tenancy of land or buildings” and disputes arising from loans etc., and generally is for consumer purchases/sales rather than building-contract performance [9].  
- The Property disputes cover does include disputes after physical damage to your home, but only where the dispute relates to an event causing physical damage and the amount in dispute is more than £100; it also requires you to have (or reasonable prospects of) legal ownership or right in the property [10]. If your dispute with the builder is about defective workmanship that has caused accidental physical damage and the amount in dispute exceeds £100, the Property disputes section may potentially apply; otherwise a pure contract dispute about workmanship is excluded [10].  
- The insurer will appoint a preferred lawyer; you may choose your own only when legal proceedings start or on conflict of interest, and if you choose your own lawyer costs above what the insurer would have paid (currently £100 per hour) may be your responsibility [3, 1].  
- The insurer will not pursue claims their appointed lawyer believes are unlikely to succeed (“more likely than not”) [7].

If you want a definitive answer for your case, you need to establish whether the builder’s poor work caused physical damage to the home (and the amount in dispute), and whether the appointed lawyer considers your prospects of success; those facts determine whether either Property disputes or Consumer disputes cover could apply [10, 9, 7].
