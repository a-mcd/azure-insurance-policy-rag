# Question

A visitor tripped on a loose paving stone at my home and was injured. Would my insurance cover me if they made a claim against me?

# Answer

Yes — your policy includes cover for a visitor’s injury if they make a claim against you under personal liability or occupier’s/property owner’s liability, subject to the policy limits and exclusions.

Explanation:
- The policy’s Personal liability section covers your legal liability after an accident that caused bodily injury to a person, provided the accident happened during the period of insurance and in the UK, Isle of Man or Channel Islands [4].  
- The Occupier’s liability section specifically covers your liability to the public as occupier of your home (which would apply to a visitor) [5].  
- The Property owner’s liability section also covers amounts you legally have to pay as owners of your home for accidental injury to any person; this is included at all levels [6].  
- Personal injury cover under the legal expenses wording is restricted to sudden or specific accidents causing bodily injury, which aligns with an accidental trip [8]; gradual injury or solely psychological injury are excluded [2, 3].  
- The policy exclusions that could affect a claim include liability arising from motor vehicles, certain animals, business or professional activities, or injuries to you or household members (not third‑party visitors) — see the listed exclusions in the relevant liability sections [4, 5, 6].  
- You must provide details of the incident, any witnesses and other requested evidence when making a claim [10].

If different facts apply (for example the injury resulted from a gradual condition, was caused by a motor vehicle, arose from a business activity, or involved someone who permanently lives with you), different exclusions may apply and cover may not be available — the excerpts don’t allow determination for those scenarios [2, 4, 5, 6].
