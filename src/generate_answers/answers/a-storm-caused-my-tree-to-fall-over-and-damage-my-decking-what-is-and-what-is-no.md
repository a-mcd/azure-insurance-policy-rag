# Question

A storm caused my tree to fall over and damage my decking. What is and what is not covered?

# Answer

Covered: The buildings section covers damage caused by falling trees and branches to the buildings (which would include fixed decking if it is insured as part of the buildings) when caused by a storm, provided the weather meets the policy’s storm definition (wind gusts ≥55mph, heavy rainfall ≥25mm/hr, snow ≥30cm in 24 hours, or damaging hail) [5, 1, 3]. The contents section also covers loss from falling trees and branches where relevant [8]. Storm cover only applies if the weather meets the policy’s storm definition; non-storm wind, rain, snow or sleet is not insured [1, 7].

Not covered: Damage to gates, fences or hedges from storm or from falling trees is excluded under the policy [2, 3, 5, 10]. The buildings cover excludes the cost of removing fallen trees or branches that have not caused damage to the buildings, and excludes the cost of removing any part of the tree remaining below ground or of restoring the site [5]. The policy also excludes loss that happens gradually through wear and tear or due to lack of maintenance, and excludes damage from rising ground-water levels or from subsidence, heave or landslip resulting from storm or flood where stated [3, 4, 5, 1].

If the decking is part of the insured buildings then falling-tree storm damage is within cover up to the buildings cover limits; if the decking is a gate, fence or similar structure that the excerpts treat like a fence/gate/hedge, or if the storm did not meet the policy’s storm definition, the damage would be excluded. The excerpts do not show the buildings sum insured, excess amounts, or whether your decking is specifically treated as buildings or as an excluded gate/fence item, so those details cannot be established from the supplied documents [3, 1, 5].
