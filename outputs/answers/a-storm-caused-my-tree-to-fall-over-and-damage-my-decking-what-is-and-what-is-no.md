# Question

A storm caused my tree to fall over and damage my decking. What is and what is not covered?

# Answer

Covered: The policy’s buildings cover includes loss or damage caused by falling trees and branches, so damage to your decking caused by a tree that fell in a storm is covered under the buildings section, provided the weather meets the policy’s storm definition (for example wind gusts of at least 55mph, heavy rainfall at least 25mm/hr, snow 30cm in 24 hours, or hail that damages hard surfaces) [3, 5, 1]. Also the contents cover separately includes falling trees and branches (if the decking were covered as contents, which is unlikely) [8].

Not covered: The policy does not cover loss or damage to gates, fences or hedges caused by storm or falling trees [3, 2, 10]. The insurer will not pay for wind/rain/snow/sleet damage unless the event meets the policy’s storm definition [7, 1]. The policy excludes damage that happens gradually through wear and tear or due to lack of maintenance, so if the decking failed because of poor maintenance rather than the storm impact that would not be covered [1, 3, 4].

Other limits and exclusions you should note: The falling-trees cover excludes loss or damage caused during tree maintenance, the cost of removing fallen trees or branches that haven’t caused damage to the buildings, and the cost of removing any part of the tree remaining below ground or restoring the site — so removal of the tree stump or removal of an undamaged tree would not be covered [5]. If the event was storm-related you may have a separate flood excess shown in your Home Policy Schedule (where relevant) [3, 4].

If different facts would change the outcome: If you cannot establish that the weather met the policy’s storm definition, or if the decking was damaged by gradual deterioration or poor maintenance rather than the falling tree in the storm, the excerpts do not support cover [1, 7, 3].
