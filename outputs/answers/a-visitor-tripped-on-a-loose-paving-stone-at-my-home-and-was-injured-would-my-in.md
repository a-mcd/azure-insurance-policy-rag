# Question

A visitor tripped on a loose paving stone at my home and was injured. Would my insurance cover me if they made a claim against me?

# Answer

Yes — your policy’s liability cover can respond if a visitor tripped on a loose paving stone and makes a claim against you, provided the accident meets the policy conditions. Your Personal liability section covers your legal liability after an accident that caused bodily injury to a person in the UK during the period of insurance [4]. Occupier’s liability (covering your liability to the public as occupier of your home) and Property owner’s liability (covering amounts you legally have to pay as owners of your home for accidental injury) are also included under all levels [5, 6]. 

Material conditions and exclusions you must check:
- The injury must be from an accident (sudden) occurring during the period of insurance and in the UK, Isle of Man or Channel Islands for Personal liability [4].
- You cannot claim for injury to you, a family member or anyone permanently living with you under these sections (they are excluded) [4, 5, 6].
- You cannot claim under both Occupier’s liability and Property owner’s liability for the same matter; use the section that applies to your situation (occupier vs owner) [5, 6].
- Certain liabilities are excluded (for example those arising from motor vehicles, professional/business activities, animals other than cats/dogs you’re responsible for, and specified other matters) which would not apply here unless relevant to the incident [4, 5, 6].

If you have Home Legal Expenses cover (standard on Platinum or optional otherwise) it can assist with personal injury legal matters arising from a specific or sudden accident, though it notes limits and excludes gradual illness or solely psychological injury [2, 8, 3]. 

You should report the claim with full incident details and any witness information as requested in the claims guidance [10].
