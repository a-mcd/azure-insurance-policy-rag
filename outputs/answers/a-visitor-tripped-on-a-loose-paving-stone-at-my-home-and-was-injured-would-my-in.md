# Question

A visitor tripped on a loose paving stone at my home and was injured. Would my insurance cover me if they made a claim against me?

# Answer

Yes — your policy can cover a claim brought against you by a visitor injured after tripping on a loose paving stone, under the Personal liability or Occupier’s liability sections, provided the accident happened in the UK/Isle of Man/Channel Islands during the period of insurance and the injured person is not you, a family member or someone permanently living with you [4, 5]. 

Explanation:
- Personal liability covers your legal liability after an accident that caused physical injury to a person (and applies in the UK, Isle of Man and the Channel Islands) [4]. 
- Occupier’s liability covers your liability to the public as occupier (but not owner) of your home, which is the most directly applicable wording for a visitor injured on your property [5]. 
- Property owner’s liability also covers amounts you legally have to pay for accidental injury as owners of your home, so it could apply if you are the owner rather than only the occupier; you can’t claim under both Occupier’s liability and Property owner’s liability simultaneously, so which section applies depends on whether you are occupier or owner (the excerpts show both covers are included) [6, 5]. 

Material exclusions and conditions you must meet:
- The injured person must not be you, a family member or someone permanently living with you — those injuries are excluded from these liability sections [4, 5, 6]. 
- The accident must be sudden (an accident) and occur during the period of insurance and within the listed territories [4, 8]. 
- If you make a claim for such liability you will need to provide details of the incident, any responsible person, witnesses and other requested information (policy number, date/time/details) as set out in the policy [10]. 

What I cannot establish from the excerpts:
- The excerpts do not state monetary limits or who would defend you or pay legal costs under these specific liability sections, nor do they state any excess amounts for liability claims — those details are not included in the provided material.
