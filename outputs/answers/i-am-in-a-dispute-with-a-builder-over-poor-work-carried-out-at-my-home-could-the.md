# Question

I am in a dispute with a builder over poor work carried out at my home. Could the policy pay my solicitor’s fees?

# Answer

No — the policy will not pay solicitor’s fees for a dispute with a builder that arises from a contract, unless different facts apply.

Explanation:
- “Consumer disputes” cover applies to contractual disputes you entered into in a personal capacity, but the policy’s “what is not covered” for Property disputes explicitly excludes “a contract you have entered into” from Property disputes [10].
- The Consumer disputes section covers contractual disputes for buying or hiring goods or services, but it requires the dispute to be one of the listed consumer types and the amount in dispute to be more than £100 [9]. The excerpts do not show that a builder contract dispute falls within the Consumer disputes covered examples, and Property disputes (which would more directly concern your home) exclude contract disputes [9, 10].
- The legal expenses policy will also not cover claims where the lawyer we appoint does not believe you are more likely than not to win [7], and small disputes under £100 are excluded [2, 9]. If you choose your own lawyer after proceedings start, any costs above what the insurer would have paid their preferred lawyers (currently £100 per hour) may be your responsibility [3, 1].
- Therefore, based on the supplied excerpts, a contractual dispute with a builder is excluded under Property disputes and is not shown as covered under Consumer disputes for this situation; so the policy would not pay your solicitor’s fees. [10, 9, 7, 2, 1, 3]

If the dispute instead met the Consumer disputes definitions in the excerpts (for example it was clearly one of the covered consumer types and over £100), the Consumer disputes section would be the relevant cover to consider [9].
