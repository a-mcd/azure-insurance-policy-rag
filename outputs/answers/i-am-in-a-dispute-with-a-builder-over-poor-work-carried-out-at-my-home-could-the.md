# Question

I am in a dispute with a builder over poor work carried out at my home. Could the policy pay my solicitor’s fees?

# Answer

No — only if the dispute fits the policy’s legal expenses cover for either a consumer dispute or a property dispute and the other cover conditions are met.

Explanation:
- If your dispute with the builder is a consumer dispute (a contractual dispute about goods or services you entered into in a personal capacity), the policy covers such disputes only where the amount in dispute is more than £100 (including VAT) and the dispute is not excluded by the listed exclusions (for example disputes arising from a loan/mortgage/investment/borrowing or disputes over sale/purchase/lease terms of land/buildings except limited adviser disputes) [9].
- If your dispute is a property dispute (a civil dispute relating to your principal home or personal possessions following physical damage, legal nuisance or trespass), the policy covers these only where the amount in dispute is more than £100 and you have or there are reasonable prospects of establishing legal ownership or right to the property; the policy does not cover disputes that are contract disputes or that concern land/buildings other than your main home [10].
- The insurer will appoint a preferred lawyer; you may choose your own lawyer only when legal proceedings start or if there is a conflict of interest, and if you do choose your own lawyer any costs above what the insurer would have paid their preferred lawyers (currently £100 per hour) may not be paid by the insurer [3, 1].
- The insurer will not fund claims where their appointed lawyer does not believe you are more likely than not to win [7].
- Additional contractual requirements and consequences (for example obtaining expert opinion if requested, recovering costs you must pay back to the insurer if recovered, and consequences of settling or withdrawing a claim without agreement) also apply [6].

What is missing / decision not possible from excerpts:
- The excerpts do not state whether your specific dispute with the builder is a consumer dispute or a property dispute, nor do they state the amount in dispute or whether the insurer’s appointed lawyer would consider your prospects of success to be more likely than not. Without those facts, it cannot be established whether the policy will pay your solicitor’s fees.
